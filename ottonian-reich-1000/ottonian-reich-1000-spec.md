# The Reich of the Ottonians, c. AD 1000 - map spec

The German kingdom and its neighbours in the year Otto III went on pilgrimage to Gniezno, opened Charlemagne's tomb at Aachen and set up new archbishoprics for Poland and Hungary. Sites and events come from the whole Ottonian age, 919 to 1024, each with its date. The physical base and the place system are built to carry two later sheets on the same frame: the Staufer Reich c. 1190 and the Hanse c. 1356.

Title cartouche: "The Reich of the Ottonians / Germany and its neighbours from the Eider to the Po / in the year of Otto III's journey to Gniezno, AD 1000".

## Frame and print

- Sheet: 48 x 36 in landscape, 14400 x 10800 px at 300 dpi, about 129.6 m per px
- Projection: Lambert Conformal Conic, lat_1 46.3, lat_2 54.3, lat_0 50.3, lon_0 11.6, WGS84
- Projected extent (m): x -820,000 to 1,046,700; y -720,000 to 680,000
- Corners: NW 1.5W 55.8N, NE 28.2E 55.4N, SW 1.5E 43.3N, SE 24.4E 43.0N; top centre 56.4N, bottom centre 43.8N
- Coverage: Flanders, Brabant and the Paris basin in the west; Ribe, Funen, Zealand and Scania in the north; the Vistula, Kraków, Sandomierz and the Cherven towns in the east; Genoa, Pavia, Venice and Ravenna in the south, with Lyon, Vienne and the Pannonian plain at the edges
- Off the sheet, named at the edge with a pointer: Rome, Kyiv, Uppsala, Novgorod, Constantinople

## Physical base

Built from the NW Europe base pipeline, re-run on the new frame at the finer scale:

- Coast, lakes, rivers: Natural Earth 10m, with OpenStreetMap land polygons along the North Sea, Baltic and Adriatic coasts
- Relief: AWS Terrain Tiles, terrarium zoom 8, with the same mountain and hill thresholds as the NW Europe base; the Alps, Carpathians, Sudetes, Ore Mountains, Bohemian Forest, Harz and the Rhenish uplands get pencil mountains with white caps above about 2,000 m
- Rivers traced from the terrain where Natural Earth breaks them, with no dotted or dashed lines

### Period water, c. 1000

- Almere: the lake behind the Vlie, larger than Lacus Flevo, still a lake and not yet the Zuiderzee
- Dollart and Jadebusen kept as land (their storm floods come in 1164 to 1277)
- Zeeland as scattered islands and salt marsh, with the Scheldt mouths of the time
- Rhine: the Kromme Rijn and Oude Rijn still running past Utrecht to Katwijk; the Lek and Waal carrying most of the water
- Small peat lakes in Holland in place of the Haarlemmermeer
- Po delta further west, with Ravenna near the sea and the Comacchio lagoons open; the Venetian lagoon with its islands
- Szczecin Lagoon, Vistula Lagoon and spit, and Curonian Lagoon at their period shapes
- Lakes: Constance, Geneva, Garda, Como, Maggiore, Balaton, Neusiedl, Müritz, Steinhuder Meer, Plön, Gopło (named, the Piast heartland)

### Marshes

Holland peat, Frisian coast, Bourtanger Moor, Teufelsmoor, Drömling, Havelland and Rhinluch, Spreewald, Oderbruch, Warthe and Netze bruchs, Biebrza, the edge of the Pripet, Hanság, the Po valli, the Flemish coastal marsh.

### Woodland

Wildwood stays the default on dry land, as on the NW Europe base, but the clearings of c. 1000 are larger: the Rhineland and Moselle, the Main valley, the Wetterau, the Hellweg, the Magdeburg Börde, the Thuringian basin, the Elbe-Saale loess, the Bohemian basin, the Danube from Regensburg to Vienna, the Upper Rhine plain, Swabia and the Lake Constance shore, Silesia and Greater Poland around Gniezno and Poznań, the Kraków basin, the Hungarian plain, the Po plain, the Paris basin and Champagne, Flanders and Brabant.

Named forests, each lettered across its full extent:
- Lotharingia and the Rhineland: Ardennes (Arduenna), Silva Carbonaria (Kohlenwald), Eifel, Hunsrück, Vosges (Vosagus), Hagenau Holy Forest, Bienwald, Pfälzerwald
- Franconia and Swabia: Spessart, Odenwald, Steigerwald, Black Forest (Silva Nigra), Swabian Alb, Nuremberg Reichswald
- Saxony and Thuringia: Harz (the royal hunting forest round Bodfeld), Solling, Reinhardswald, Teutoburg Forest, Sachsenwald, Göhrde, Thuringian Forest, the Lüneburg heath
- The east: Miriquidi (Thietmar's dark wood of the Ore Mountains), the Bohemian border forest (Nordwald), Bavarian Forest, the Silesian border forest (Przesieka), Tuchola Forest, Notecka Forest, Kampinos, Białowieża
- The south: Wienerwald, Ebersberger Forst

## Polities

No borders. Each kingdom, duchy, march and people is lettered across the land that bore its name in c. 1000, in a size set by its rank:

- Kingdoms and the Empire, large spaced capitals: Regnum Teutonicum, Regnum Italiae, Regnum Burgundiae, Francia Occidentalis, Bohemia, Polonia (with Civitas Schinesghe beneath, from the Dagome iudex), Ungaria, Danmark
- Duchies, medium capitals: Saxonia, Franconia, Upper and Lower Lotharingia, Suevia (Alamannia), Baioaria, Carinthia, Thuringia; Flanders, Champagne, Burgundy; Lombardy, Tuscany at the bottom edge, the Ivrea and Turin marches
- Marches, smaller capitals: Billung March, Northern March, March of Meissen, Lusatian Mark, Ostarrichi (named in the charter of 996), March of Verona, Friuli, Carniola
- Peoples, italic capitals: Obodrites, Wagrians, Polabians, Lutici (with the Redarii, Tollensi, Circipani and Kessini), Rani, Hevelli, Sorbs, Milceni, Pomeranians, Prussians, Vistulans, Slenzanes, Moravians, Croats at the south-east edge, Frisians
- The lands east of the Elbe lost in the Slav rising of 983 are lettered with a note: "lost to the Lutici, 983"
- Colour edition: the name colour shows allegiance (Reich, Italy and Burgundy under the emperor; Bohemia and Poland bound to him; Denmark, West Francia and Hungary independent; pagan Slav and Baltic peoples). Parchment edition: ink in graded strengths

## Places: graded symbols

The symbols follow the logic of Helwig's 1561 Silesia key (Stadt, Closter, Schlos, Dorf): small hand-drawn vignettes for the important places, simple marks for the rest. They are drawn in the same hand as the NW Europe library.

Vignettes, graded by size:
1. Imperial city and seat of the court (Aachen, Magdeburg, Quedlinburg, Regensburg, Pavia): walled town with a crowned tower
2. Archbishopric (Mainz, Cologne, Trier, Magdeburg, Salzburg, Hamburg-Bremen, Gniezno, Esztergom, Aquileia, Milan, Ravenna, Reims, Besançon, Lyon, Vienne): walled town with a double-cross spire
3. Bishopric: town with a single cross spire. All sees in place c. 1000, from Utrecht, Liège and Cambrai to Prague, Olomouc, Poznań, Kraków, Wrocław and Kołobrzeg, the Danish sees of Schleswig, Ribe and Aarhus, and the sees of northern Italy. Brandenburg, Havelberg and Oldenburg are marked as sees in exile after 983
4. Civitas (walled town of Roman origin with no see) and great trading town (Haithabu, Wolin, Venice): walled town with gate towers
5. Royal palace (Pfalz): hall with a single tower. Ingelheim, Frankfurt, Tribur, Werla, Pöhlde, Memleben, Tilleda, Allstedt, Grone, Dortmund, Duisburg, Nijmegen, Bodfeld, Wallhausen, Merseburg and the others in the itineraries
6. Imperial abbey or great monastery: church with a cross. Fulda, Corvey, Reichenau, St Gall, Lorsch, Hersfeld, Prüm, Echternach, Werden, Essen, Gandersheim, Gernrode, Nienburg, Einsiedeln, Tegernsee, Niederaltaich, St Emmeram, Bobbio, Nonantola, Pomposa, Cluny

Marks:
7. Market or wik (Tiel, Deventer, Bardowick, Huy, Dinant, Namur, Maastricht and others): small house with a market cross
8. Emporium on the sea (Ribe, Ralswiek, Menzlin, Truso, Szczecin, Gdańsk, Wiskiauten): the market mark with a small ship beneath
9. Castle or burgward: tower on a mound
10. Slav or Magyar stronghold (Gniezno, Ostrów Lednicki, Giecz, Brennaburg, Arkona, Rethra, Starigard, Libice, Budeč, Levý Hradec, Wrocław): round rampart with a palisade
11. Danish ring fortress (Trelleborg, Fyrkat, Aggersborg, Nonnebakken, Borgring) and the Danevirke: quartered ring and a ticked earthwork line
12. Village or lesser place: dot with a small cross (Helwig's Dorf)
13. Ruin or abandoned site (Dorestad, Quentovic, Reric): broken outline of the market mark

Signs placed beside the place symbol:
- Mint (Otto-Adelheid pennies and the other Ottonian mints)
- Mine: Rammelsberg silver at Goslar (from c. 968), Harz and Bohemian silver
- Salt: Halle, Lüneburg, Reichenhall, Hallein, Hallstatt, Schwäbisch Hall, Bad Nauheim, Kołobrzeg, Bochnia
- Battle with its year, 919 to 1000: Brennaburg 928/29, Lenzen 929, Riade 933, Birten 939, Andernach 939, Lechfeld 955, the Recknitz 955, Cedynia 972, the sack of Havelberg and Brandenburg 983

### The later Hanse layer

Each town that later joined the Hanse gets a small faint cog beside its name, with the year of first record as a member, so the same place data can carry the 1190 and 1356 sheets. Towns that do not yet exist in 1000 are shown under their forerunner (Liubice for Lübeck, Gyddanyzc for Danzig, Bruges as a Flemish market). The full Hanse network belongs on the 1356 sheet.

## Names

The form used in the records of the time, usually the Latin of Thietmar, Widukind and the diplomas, or the vernacular where that is how the place is known (Magadaburg, Colonia, Mogontia, Treveris, Aquisgrani, Hammaburg, Brennaburg, Misni, Praga, Gnezdun, Cracova, Strigonium, Ratispona, Papia). The modern name sits beneath in small italic.

## Routes and movements

- Otto III's journey of 1000 as a dark red dashed line with arrowheads: Rome, Ravenna, Regensburg, Zeitz, Meissen, Gniezno, Magdeburg, Quedlinburg, Aachen
- Adalbert's mission to the Prussians, 997, ending at his martyrdom
- Magyar raids before 955 as green dashed arrows, ending at the Lechfeld
- Main ways cut as narrow lanes through the woods: the Hellweg, the Rhine and Danube roads, Regensburg to Prague and Kraków (Ibrahim ibn Yaqub's route), the Raffelstetten salt road, the Ochsenweg in Jutland, and the Alpine passes (Brenner, Reschen, Septimer, Julier, Great St Bernard, Mont Cenis), each pass named

## Quotations

Six to eight short passages in italic near the places they describe, each with its source: Thietmar on the Miriquidi and on Gniezno in 1000, Widukind on the Lechfeld, Ibrahim ibn Yaqub on Prague and on Mainz, Adam of Bremen on Wolin (Jumne), and Liudprand of Cremona.

- Title cartouche in the North Sea

## Lettering

As on the NW Europe sheets: Fondamento capitals for polities and peoples on gentle curves, MedievalSharp for places beside the symbol with the modern name beneath in italic, period names for seas, rivers, forests and mountains. Rivers and forests are named before polities so they always find room. All lettering is converted to outlines.

## Editions

- Parchment: aged paper, no sea or land fill, ink rivers, muted woods, white only on the peaks, polity names in graded ink
- Colour: the painted scheme of the NW Europe sheets (deep sea with pale wave rings, land tinted by height, trees one step darker), with polity names coloured by allegiance
- Key, title, sources, inset and border stay on parchment in both

## Later sheets on the same base

- Staufer Reich, c. 1190: same frame; Jadebusen opening, the Zuiderzee starting to form; clearings widened for the eastward settlement; Lübeck, Staufer castles and palaces, the Italian communes
- The Hanse, c. 1356: same physical base, but the frame may need to reach Riga, Visby and Bergen; Zuiderzee and Dollart open; the full Hanse network
